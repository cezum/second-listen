# Post-Investment Risk Playbook

This reusable template turns post-investment monitoring into an auditable operating loop: **set up the project -> collect evidence -> compare with the agreed baseline -> review exceptions -> decide actions -> update the ledger**.

All thresholds and trigger examples are placeholders. Each institution must replace them with its own policy, approval chain, and legal requirements. This template is not investment advice.

## Operating depth

Start with the smallest workflow that can be kept current, then add data and controls as the process proves useful.

| Mode | Coverage | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Core | Interview notes, evidence capture, human risk assessment, and ledger updates | Immediate day-to-day use |
| Standard | Core workflow plus budget and prior-period comparisons and exception review | Projects with reliable operating data |
| Full | Standard workflow plus investment-case baselines, escalation reports, and exit or remediation planning | Material or closely monitored projects |

## Evidence collection

Create a project card with the project identifier, sector, investment date and amount, ownership, responsible people, agreement terms, performance commitments, repurchase or compensation triggers, monitoring baselines, important dates, and source documents.

Collect periodic financials, operating updates, interview or visit notes, board materials, public filings, litigation, ownership changes, senior-team changes, major contracts, and other policy-defined events. For every extracted fact, retain the source, date, confidence, and reviewer status.

AI may extract fields and draft records. A person must verify the source documents before a record becomes authoritative.

## Exception review

Use institution-approved thresholds as prompts, never as automatic decisions. Review adverse indicators alongside mitigating context, including policy objectives, market conditions, guarantees, agreed remedies, and management responses.

Typical escalation triggers include key-person loss, funds used outside the agreed purpose, major litigation or safety events, ownership or control changes, missed performance commitments, delayed listing plans, material over-disbursement, and major adverse policy or market changes.

AI can identify candidates, cite supporting evidence, and suggest a next question. It must not silently trigger a report, change a risk decision, or replace the authorized reviewer.

## Evidence dimensions

Review the dimensions that matter for the institution and project. Common dimensions are operations, exit path, self-funding, team integrity, and financial health. Keep evidence separate from the decision: the record should show what happened, where it came from, who reviewed it, and what action was approved.

## Human decision and actions

The project team may prepare an initial assessment. Risk, compliance, or another independent reviewer checks the evidence. The authorized person confirms the decision and rationale. Record every change with its date, trigger, evidence, reviewer, and next verification point.

For a material exception, prepare the institution's required report: cause, event description, current response, effect on the investment objective, corrective action, owner, and deadline. If remediation or exit is considered, follow the original decision process and applicable legal controls.

## Ledger and cadence

Keep one row per project with the latest data period, evidence status, key events, open actions, owners, deadlines, verification dates, and last update. Review the ledger regularly, refresh operating data on the institution's cadence, and reassess when a material event occurs. Archive closed projects separately without losing their audit trail.

## AI boundary

AI extracts, compares, cites, drafts, and asks. People verify, decide, approve, sign, escalate, and own the resulting action. Never present an AI suggestion as a confirmed decision.
