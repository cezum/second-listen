Second Listen

“You said it out loud. You didn’t realize it was a signal.”

A voice agent for post-investment risk management.

Team: cezum · MIT-licensed · Built on AssemblyAI
01
The gap

Every fund tracks metrics.
Nobody captures what they hear.

Dashboard signals

Revenue decline, runway under six months, covenant breaches. Visible in KPI reports, already covered by portfolio monitoring tools.

Conversation signals

A CFO leaving, grant money quietly moved to payroll, a lawsuit mentioned in passing. They exist only in conversation.

Second Listen covers the second channel — the gap between “the conversation happened” and “the signal is in the ledger”.
02
Why voice, why now

A signal is freshest at the moment
it is spoken.

By the time a quarterly review form gets filled, memory has eaten half the evidence — the quote, the date, who said it, what was promised.

What survives is a summary nobody can audit. What is lost is exactly the thing that should have triggered escalation.

Talking right after the call is the lowest-friction moment to capture it.
03
How it works

A debrief that ends
in evidence, not notes.

  1. Debrief — after a founder call, board meeting or site visit, press and talk at a natural pace, casually retelling what happened.
  2. Interrogate — the agent follows the playbook’s escalation checklist, one question at a time: who covers finance since the CFO left, was there written approval for moving the grant money.
  3. Record — an evidence table with timestamped quotes, a risk-ledger update, escalation flags, and a follow-up note with an owner and a deadline.
04
The differentiator

Generic models summarize.
This one knows what to ask.

We took a real post-investment risk playbook — evidence dimensions, an escalation checklist, and human confirmation — and compiled it into skills the agent runs live.

  • File every signal with the quote it came from.
  • Escalate anything that crosses a red line in the checklist.
  • Turn each answer into an action with an owner and a due date.
The playbook is open and MIT-licensed. The agent is the slot — the method is the product.
05
Live path — what the video shows

Everything in the demo is a real run.

The 3:44 submission video is one uncut second check-in: the agent reopens last week’s follow-ups one at a time, then files what is new.

  • Prior follow-ups reopened by the agent at the opening, not by the investor.
  • Signal filed with its quote — evidence you can audit back to the second it was said.
  • Agreed action created with owner, due date and agreed-at timestamp — two reminders set on the same call.
No scripted sample playback. Live path = AssemblyAI Voice Agent API.
06
Cross-debrief memory

Next week, it opens with
last week’s promises.

Each company keeps one JSON history file — no database, no vector store. Commitments carry an owner and a deadline; recorded signals stop the agent from asking twice.

“Before we cover anything new — let’s close one prior follow-up at a time.”
After a live debrief, the history file is rebuilt from the session’s own tool calls.
07
Upload path

Same playbook, on a recording.

Not every debrief happens live. Upload a recorded call or a voice memo: Universal-2 transcribes it and the same playbook returns a signal list, escalation flags and follow-up questions.

Chinese recordings work today. The live path can listen to Chinese and other languages but replies in English. Upload analysis keeps the recording’s language in its transcript and results.

Demo case: an uploaded debrief returns 9 signals, 7 to review, 9 open questions — including a compliance red line and funds used outside the agreed purpose.
08
Technology

One WebSocket for the live path.

Live debrief

AssemblyAI Voice Agent API — STT + LLM + TTS, turn detection, barge-in and JSON-schema tool calling over a single connection.

Upload analysis

AssemblyAI Universal-2 transcription plus a pluggable OpenAI-compatible LLM. Same playbook, two paths.

The entire backend is Python standard library only — requirements.txt installs nothing.
09
Business value

Who pays, and why.

The buyer is the post-investment or risk function of a fund or an investment platform — the team that already owns a risk process on paper, but has no way to feed it from conversations.

Post-investment is the first slot, not the only one. The same slot accepts a legal case checklist, a credit due-diligence script or a safety inspection list.

AI surfaces evidence and a suggested assessment. Humans make the final risk decision — the playbook’s own rule, kept intact.
10
Originality

The hard part was never
transcription.

Transcription is solved. Knowing what matters, what needs follow-up, and what you are exposed to if nobody wrote it down — that is not.

Second Listen is a domain playbook compiled into a voice agent: file the signal, cite the quote, chase the owner. Open method, running on AssemblyAI.

Everything from this call is now on the record: the signals, the quote behind each one, and the follow-ups — each with an owner and a due date.
